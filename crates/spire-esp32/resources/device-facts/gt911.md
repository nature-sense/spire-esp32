# GT911 — protocol facts

Part number `gt911` (Goodix GT911), I²C capacitive touch controller. It answers at `0x5D`, or at
`0x14` when the reset/address strap selects the other value. It is the **near miss** of the
`ft6336`: the same job on the same kind of panel, and a different protocol in every detail that
matters — 16-bit register addresses, little-endian coordinates, and a status register whose low
nibble counts points.

Derived from Espressif's `esp_lcd_touch_gt911` driver and Goodix's GT911 datasheet. An
**implementation, not the datasheet**: enough to write a driver that works, not enough to be
authoritative.

## Registers

Addresses are **16-bit and sent high byte first**, then the data.

- `0x8140` `PRODUCT_ID` — 4 bytes, ASCII `"911"` followed by `0`; read it to prove the part
- `0x8144` `FIRMWARE_VERSION` — 2 bytes
- `0x814E` `BUFFER_STATUS` — bit 7 = a report is ready; bits 3:0 = points down; write `0` after
  reading to clear it
- `0x8150` `POINT1` — 8 bytes: track id, `X[7:0]`, `X[15:8]`, `Y[7:0]`, `Y[15:8]`, size, reserved,
  reserved

Coordinates are **little-endian 16-bit** (`low | high << 8`), not the FT6336's high-nibble-first
twelve bits, and the addresses are two bytes, not one. A driver written against one of these two
parts does not talk to the other.

## A reading

Read `0x814E`. If bit 7 is clear there is nothing new. Otherwise take `bits 3:0` as the point count
(`0` = a release), read `0x8150` for the first point, then write `0x814E = 0` to clear the ready
flag — without that the controller reports the same point forever.

## What a bad reply looks like

- `0x814E` bit 7 clear → no new report; a successful read, not a failure.
- product id is not `"911"` → not a GT911; do not decode points.
- the bus refuses the exchange → `false`, distinct from "no finger".
